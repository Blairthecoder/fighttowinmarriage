How to Fight for Your Marriage (Without Fighting Your Spouse)

By Terrie Beeman · October 19, 2026

Part of our guide: Christian Marriage Restoration

When a marriage is hurting, it is easy to start keeping score. Who said what. Who started it. Who is more wrong. But winning an argument and winning your marriage are two very different things. Fighting for your marriage means you stop fighting each other and start fighting for each other.

The short answer: to fight for your marriage, name the real enemy (it isn't your spouse), change your own part first, pray for your spouse daily, set rules for how you handle conflict, and refuse to fight alone. If your spouse isn't fighting with you right now, you can still stand, and many marriages are restored because one spouse refused to give up.

Why we call it a fight

Fight to Win Marriages started in a crisis. When Terrie Beeman's husband told her he wanted out, she did what most of us would do. She argued, she pleaded and she tried to fix him. Then the Holy Spirit showed her that what she was facing was a spiritual battle, and that her husband was not the enemy. That changed how she fought. She stood for her marriage for 365 days, and God restored it. Today she and her husband have been married 34 years. Read Terrie's story.

The ministry is built on two verses:

"For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places."Ephesians 6:12 (KJV)
"For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty through God to the pulling down of strong holds."2 Corinthians 10:4 (KJV)

Fighting for your marriage means fighting the right enemy with the right weapons. Here's what that looks like in everyday life.

How to fight for your marriage: 9 biblical steps

1. Remember who the real enemy is

Stress, exhaustion, old wounds, temptation and spiritual opposition all show up in the kitchen as an argument about the dishes. When you name the real battle, you can face it together instead of facing each other. Next time tension rises, ask yourself: "What are we actually fighting about, and who benefits if we turn on each other?"

2. Start with your own heart

You can't change your spouse. You can let God change you. Pray Psalm 139:23-24: "Search me, O God, and know my heart." Ask Him to show you any pride, resentment, bitterness or unforgiveness you're carrying. This isn't taking the blame for your spouse's choices. It's taking responsibility for the part you control. For a guided version, take the spiritual heart check.

3. Attack the problem, not the person

Street fighting has no rules: name-calling, bringing up the past, threatening divorce. Fighting fair does. Agree on a few ground rules, even if you have to keep them alone for a while:

  • Use "I feel" statements instead of "You always" accusations.
  • Stay on one issue at a time.
  • Never threaten divorce in an argument.
  • Keep conflict away from the children.
  • Either of you can call a 20-minute cool-off break, as long as you come back to the conversation.

4. Look for the win-win

If one of you wins and the other loses, the marriage loses. Ask, "What would a good outcome look like for both of us?" Real resolution takes listening, yielding and humility from both sides. "Let nothing be done through strife or vainglory; but in lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves" (Philippians 2:3).

5. Pray for your spouse, not just about them

It is hard to stay bitter toward someone you are praying blessing over. Pray for your spouse's heart, health, purpose and walk with God every day. Then pray for your own heart to soften. If you don't know what to say, start with our prayers for your marriage or the spiritual warfare prayer for marriage.

6. Add fasting to your prayers

Some breakthroughs come through prayer and fasting. Fasting doesn't earn God's favor or force your spouse to change. It quiets everything else so you can hear God, and it says, "Lord, this matters more to me than food." Start simple with one meal or one day. Read Praying and Fasting for Your Marriage for a step-by-step guide.

7. Guard your words

"Death and life are in the power of the tongue" (Proverbs 18:21). Stop rehearsing your spouse's failures to friends, family and social media. Speak life over your spouse, even when they aren't in the room. What you say about your marriage shapes how you see it.

8. Guard the gates of your home

Be careful about what you watch, who you take advice from, and what you let into your home and your heart. Friends who say "just leave him" or "you deserve better" may love you, but they aren't fighting for your marriage. Surround yourself with people who will pray with you.

9. Don't fight alone

Couples who make it through hard seasons rarely do it in isolation. Invite prayer partners, a coach or a trusted pastor into the battle. That's exactly why Terrie hosts the free Tuesday marriage prayer and fasting call on Zoom every Tuesday at 6:00 PM CT.

What to stop doing right now

Fighting for your marriage is as much about what you stop as what you start:

  • Stop chasing and pleading. Begging usually pushes a withdrawn spouse further away.
  • Stop investigating every move. Get the truth you need, then hand the rest to God.
  • Stop keeping score. A ledger of wrongs leaves no room for grace (1 Corinthians 13:5).
  • Stop making permanent decisions on your worst day. Pain is a poor counselor.
  • Stop neglecting yourself. Eat, sleep, see friends and stay close to God. A worn-out soldier can't stand.

How to fight for your marriage when your spouse won't

Sometimes only one of you is fighting. Your spouse may have checked out, moved out, or said they want a divorce. You can't force someone to stay, but you can still stand:

  • Change the atmosphere, not your spouse. Be kind, calm and consistent. Let your spouse see the change instead of hearing about it.
  • Give space without giving up. Stop pushing for big talks. Keep praying in private.
  • Get support for yourself. Christian marriage coaching works even if your spouse won't join.
  • Keep your hope in God, not in a timeline. Terrie stood for a full year before she saw her husband come home.

For a full guide, read How to Save Your Marriage Alone. If your spouse has asked for a divorce, read My Husband Wants a Divorce and I Don't or My Wife Wants a Divorce and I Don't.

Fighting for your marriage after an affair

Infidelity changes the fight. You need the truth, time to heal and real repentance from your spouse before trust can grow back. Forgiveness is a choice you make. Trust is something your spouse has to rebuild over time. Start with our healing after infidelity guide and Can a Marriage Survive Infidelity?.

Your first 30 days

  1. Week 1, get quiet. Pray daily for your spouse and yourself. Take the "is my marriage worth saving" quiz to see where you are.
  2. Week 2, look inward. Work through the heart check. Write down one thing God is asking you to change, and start.
  3. Week 3, fast and stand with others. Choose a simple fast and join the Tuesday prayer call.
  4. Week 4, get a plan. Schedule a confidential talk with a Christian marriage coach and decide your next steps with wise counsel.

When fighting for your marriage isn't safe

Standing for your marriage never means staying in danger. If your spouse hits, threatens, controls or frightens you, your first fight is for your safety and your children's. Read When Standing Isn't Safe, and call 911 or the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233 if you're in danger.

Frequently asked questions

How do you fight for your marriage biblically?

Remember that your spouse is not the enemy (Ephesians 6:12), let God search your own heart first, pray and fast for your spouse, set fair rules for conflict, guard your words, and invite godly support instead of fighting alone.

Can I fight for my marriage if my spouse has given up?

Yes. Many marriages are restored because one spouse kept standing. Focus on what you can control: your heart, your words, your prayers and your support. Give your spouse space without giving up, and get coaching for yourself.

How long should I fight for my marriage?

There is no set timeline. Terrie Beeman stood for her marriage for 365 days before it was restored. Seek God and wise counsel about your situation, and never stay in a place where you or your children are unsafe.

What should I stop doing when fighting for my marriage?

Stop pleading and chasing, stop investigating every move, stop keeping score, stop venting about your spouse to everyone, and stop making permanent decisions on your worst days.

Is fighting for your marriage the same as staying in an abusive one?

No. Fighting for your marriage never means accepting abuse. If you are afraid of your spouse, safety comes first. Call 911 or the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233.
