Trust Me. I Know What I'm Doing.

November 10, 2026 · Speaker: Terrie Beeman, Fight to Win Marriages · Edited recap, not a verbatim transcript

"This sickness is not unto death, but for the glory of God, that the Son of God may be glorified through it."John 11:4 (NKJV)

Supporting Scriptures

  • John 11:6: Jesus waited before going to Lazarus
  • John 11:14-15: Jesus plainly acknowledged that Lazarus was dead
  • John 11:25-26: Jesus speaks of resurrection and life
  • John 11:40-44: Lazarus is called out of the tomb

Theme: Marriage Restoration and Faith Through Delays

  • Marriage restoration
  • Faith through delays
  • Trusting God's timing
  • John 11

Session overview

Using the raising of Lazarus in John 11, Terrie Beeman encouraged husbands and wives to trust God when answers, change, or restoration seem delayed. The teaching emphasized that a difficult season is not proof of abandonment: God can use honest reflection, prayer, fasting, and patient faith to reveal His glory and bring life to situations that appear beyond repair.

Five key teachings

  1. Your spouse is not the enemy. Spiritual warfare is fought with prayer, fasting, and Scripture, not retaliation, contempt, or harmful counsel.
  2. Take the crisis to Jesus first. Mary and Martha sent word to Jesus. Couples were urged to run to the throne before running to the phone.
  3. Delay can carry divine purpose. Jesus waited before going to Lazarus. A delayed answer may become the setting for deeper faith, healing, and a testimony.
  4. Faith tells the truth. Jesus plainly acknowledged that Lazarus was dead. Couples can name lost trust, cold communication, pain, or unresolved wounds while still believing change is possible.
  5. God can call dead things to life. What looks final to people does not intimidate God. His timing may take months or years, but present conditions do not have to dictate a marriage's future.

When restoration feels delayed

  • Pause: Refuse to let urgency push you into destructive words or decisions.
  • Pray: Invite God into what is beyond your control and ask Him to fight, and win, the battle.
  • Examine: Ask what God may be healing or changing in you, including childhood wounds, bitterness, fear, or unhealthy patterns.
  • Persevere: Keep seeking God, studying Scripture, fasting, believing, and taking wise, constructive steps.

Personal Reflection Questions

  1. What part of my marriage currently feels delayed, lifeless, or beyond my control?
  2. Am I interpreting God's delay as abandonment? What might He be developing in us?
  3. What painful reality or root issue needs to be acknowledged honestly?
  4. What would it look like to see this season through God's perspective?
  5. What is one faithful, constructive step I can take this week?

Prayer Focus and Next Steps

  • Pray for renewed communication, trust, intimacy, prayer, and love in marriages that feel cold or broken.
  • Read John 11 this week, giving special attention to verses 4, 6, 14-15, 25-26, and 40-44.
  • Ask God: "What are You teaching me? What are You doing in me and in my spouse?"
  • Continue the Tuesday water-only fast until 3:00 p.m., as able, and join prayer at 6:00 p.m. Central.
  • Seek wise pastoral, counseling, or professional support when wounds or patterns require additional care.
Lord, help us see our marriage through Your eyes. Show us what needs healing, give us courage to face the truth, and keep us faithful while we wait. Breathe life into every broken place, strengthen our covenant, and use our story for Your glory. In Jesus' name, amen.

A note on safety: Waiting on God does not mean staying in an abusive or unsafe situation. If you or your children are in danger, get safe first and call 911 or the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233.
